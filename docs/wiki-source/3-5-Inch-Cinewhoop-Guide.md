# 3.5-Inch Cinewhoop — Filming Use Cases, Payload and Trade-offs

![BETAFPV Pavo35 cinewhoop frame photo](https://betafpv.com/cdn/shop/files/d2e1b9aec379546f426ce88c187b9234_7825ca02-2d37-40da-a299-8051a920ac4c.jpg?v=1777538418&width=1445)

*Real frame example: BETAFPV Pavo35. The manufacturer lists a 148 mm wheelbase, 92 mm ducts, 113.9 g bare-frame weight, and support for specified camera/video payload options. [Official frame specification](https://betafpv.com/products/pavo35-brushless-whoop-frame).*

A **cinewhoop** is defined more by its close-proximity filming purpose and often guarded/ducted propellers than by one universal propeller size. “3.5-inch” is common shorthand; the Pavo35 product uses **D90S-3 90 mm props** and 92 mm ducts, so always quote the exact frame and prop model.

## 1. When it makes sense

A cinewhoop can be useful for planned slow/controlled camera movements around architecture or in a carefully managed indoor set, where guards and a protected camera mount help with the shot. It is not automatically safe to fly near people, animals, traffic or fragile objects; choose a lawful site and keep safe separation.

## 3. Why ducts change the aircraft

- **Prop contact:** guards may reduce some direct prop contact but do not eliminate injury or impact risk.
- **Drag and thrust:** ducts change airflow and usually add mass; the same motor/prop/tune may behave differently from an open-prop quad.
- **Payload:** digital air unit, camera cage, action camera, ND filters, antennas and mounting hardware add mass and can alter battery/temperature needs.
- **Inertia:** a heavier aircraft with a camera payload has different stopping distance and acceleration than a light micro.
- **Maintenance:** duct cracks/rivets, camera vibration isolation, mount screws and wire routing need inspection after contact or crash.
- **Thermal care:** digital VTX/air units can require airflow, and some manuals limit stationary bench operation.

## 4. Choosing camera and video gear

A cinewhoop may use an analog camera/VTX, digital air unit/camera, or an independent action camera depending on frame support and use case. Check:
- Physical camera size (e.g. 19/20 mm class) and cage/tilt.
- Exact digital air-unit camera and goggles/VRX compatibility.
- Live FPV image vs onboard recording resolution and mode.
- VTX input voltage, power draw, cooling, mounting and antenna protection.
- Camera payload and battery impact on complete takeoff weight.

Do not assume DJI consumer camera drones are modular FPV components. For DIY aircraft check [Analog vs Digital FPV Cameras and Video](Analog-vs-Digital-FPV-Camera-Video-Guide.md) and the specific [DJI](DJI-O3-O4-Goggles-and-Air-Unit-Integration.md), [Walksnail](Walksnail-Avatar-Goggles-and-Air-Unit-Integration.md), or [HDZero](HDZero-Goggles-and-VTX-Integration.md) integration guide.

## 5. Assembly and payload planning

1. Check exact frame revision and manual; identify duct, arm, stack, camera and action-camera mount parts.
2. Verify motor screw length and motor/prop model; never allow a screw to touch motor windings.
3. Mount FC/ESC and air unit with required standoffs, electrical isolation and airflow. Use the exact board drawing.
4. Install the camera/gimbal and confirm lens FOV, cable reach and vibration isolation.
5. Route antennas and wires away from props and ducts, and protect coax from pinch/bend.
6. Fit battery retention; check centre of gravity and that straps cannot contact props.
7. Inspect ducts and full prop clearance with power disconnected.
8. Complete electronics/firmware checks with props removed, then install the verified props last.

Use [3.5-Inch Cinewhoop Frame Assembly](3-5-Inch-Cinewhoop-Frame-Assembly.md) and [FPV Assembly Tools and Workshop Guide](FPV-Assembly-Tools-and-Workshop-Guide.md).

## 6. Who should choose it?

Choose a cinewhoop when a planned shot benefits from the guard/camera arrangement and you have space, training, a safe operational plan and budget for camera/video hardware. Consider an open-prop micro or 5-inch freestyle build instead if your main priority is race performance, high-energy tricks, maximum efficiency, or low repair cost.
