# Simulator Training

Aim for smooth, repeatable control rather than speed. Practise for 30–45 minutes at a time; stop when fatigue makes inputs sloppy.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mandatory beginner video course before your first real flight:** complete the [Learn to fly an FPV drone (for total beginners) playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLwoDb7WF6c8lCKhQOTy-Vb9LfW0VAIrTP) in sequence while practising in Liftoff. This is a buildFPV learning requirement, **not an official licence or certification**; continue to check safety requirements and current local rules.


## Session pattern

- 5 min: warm-up, gentle inputs, check controller behavior.
- 10 min: one focused skill, such as throttle control or yaw turns.
- 10–15 min: repeat the same drill with consistent settings.
- 5–10 min: combine skills in a simple circuit or figure-eight.
- 5 min: land, review crashes and log the next task.

## Drills in order

1. **Axis awareness:** identify roll, pitch, yaw and throttle; make small controlled inputs.
2. **Straight flight:** accelerate gently, maintain heading, reduce throttle, and settle the aircraft.
3. **Turn coordination:** practise broad turns in both directions without abrupt yaw corrections.
4. **Controlled circles:** maintain a consistent altitude and line.
5. **Figure-eight:** link left and right turns without rushing the crossover.
6. **Gate approach:** choose a line well before the gate; avoid last-second stick jolts.
7. **Landing/recovery:** return to open space, reduce speed and land deliberately.

## Practice rules

- Keep the same rates and craft preset while learning a skill; change them only as a deliberate experiment.
- Start in a forgiving open area. Tight gaps and racing are later exercises.
- After a crash, describe what happened before restarting. Choose one correction for the next attempt.
- Repeat a drill until it works reliably across several attempts, then progress.

## Ready to progress when

- [ ] Stick axes and throttle behavior are predictable.
- [ ] You can take off, fly a broad circuit and land repeatedly.
- [ ] You can complete a simple figure-eight without rushing corrections.
- [ ] You can identify why most crashes happened and select a relevant drill.
- [ ] You can recover calmly to open space and disarm/land rather than chasing speed.
