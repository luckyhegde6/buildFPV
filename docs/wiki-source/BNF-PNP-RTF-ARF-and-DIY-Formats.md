# BNF, PNP, ARF, RTF and DIY — What Is Actually Included?

![Comparison of common FPV purchase formats and typical kit contents](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luckyhegde6/buildFPV/main/docs/assets/bnf-pnp-rtf-arf-comparison.svg)

These acronyms describe **purchase/package completion**, not the aircraft's size or flying style. A 2.5-inch micro, tinywhoop, cinewhoop or 5-inch quad can be sold in different package formats. Read the exact SKU's “what's included” list; manufacturers use package terms somewhat differently.

## 1. Package formats

| Label | Usually means | What you must confirm |
|---|---|---|
| **BNF — Bind-N-Fly** | Assembled aircraft with an installed receiver, generally ready to bind/configure | Exact receiver protocol and frequency, transmitter compatibility, goggles/video equipment, battery, charger and remaining setup work |
| **PNP — Plug-N-Play** | Aircraft is assembled but may omit the control receiver and/or other gear | The package contents vary. Confirm receiver, transmitter module, battery, charger, goggles, antenna and any required assembly |
| **RTF — Ready-To-Fly** | A bundle intended to include most equipment needed for initial flight | Does it include transmitter, goggles, batteries, charger, cables and spare props? A kit may still require setup, charging and safety checks |
| **ARF / ARF+ — Almost Ready to Fly** | Partially assembled aircraft or kit that requires additional components/work | There is no universal inclusion list; soldering, receiver, battery, video system and final assembly may be required |
| **DIY / self-build** | You select compatible frame, FC/ESC, motors, props, receiver, video, battery and accessories | Requires compatibility review, mechanical assembly, soldering if applicable, firmware/configuration and troubleshooting |
| **Kit** | A collection of parts, often with build instructions | The word “kit” does not say which components or tools are included; use the actual list |

## 2. BNF does not mean universally compatible

For a BNF aircraft, check:
- Receiver protocol, frequency band, firmware version and binding procedure.
- Whether your transmitter's internal/external module supports that protocol/version.
- Whether the flight controller's receiver configuration is already suitable for the installed receiver.
- Whether you own a compatible FPV video receiver/goggles system. BNF does not guarantee the goggles are included or compatible with your existing gear.
- Battery chemistry, cell count, connector, charger, required spares and supported firmware target.

An ELRS 2.4 GHz transmitter will not bind to an unrelated FrSky or proprietary receiver merely because both are “radio control.” Verify exact protocol and firmware.

## 3. Budget the entire system

Even a BNF purchase may need:
- Compatible radio transmitter / module.
- Matching FPV goggles or VRX, antennas and any required battery/cables.
- Correct batteries, a supported balance charger and safe storage/transport.
- Spare propellers, ducts/arms/canopy and fasteners.
- Simulator time, tools, maintenance and likely crash repairs.

A DIY build may cost more in tools and time even if its parts total looks comparable. It can teach much more about the aircraft and make repairs easier, but compatibility errors can destroy components or create unsafe wiring.
