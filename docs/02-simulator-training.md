# 2. Liftoff simulator training

The objective is not to win races immediately. It is to develop smooth, repeatable control and learn recovery habits without risking hardware.

> For reusable tick-box checks, see [Practical FPV checklists](checklists.md).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mandatory beginner video course before your first real flight:** complete the [Learn to fly an FPV drone (for total beginners) playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLwoDb7WF6c8lCKhQOTy-Vb9LfW0VAIrTP) in sequence while practising in Liftoff. This is a buildFPV learning requirement, **not an official licence or certification**; continue to check safety requirements and current local rules.

## Before the first session

- Connect the RadioMaster Pocket by USB-C to the computer.
- Follow the radio's on-screen USB-mode prompt if one appears; choose the joystick/HID-style mode when offered for simulator control rather than storage.
- In the operating system and Liftoff's controller/input settings, confirm the controller is detected and calibrate it.
- Verify roll, pitch, yaw, and throttle individually. Check that throttle is at minimum when the stick is at its physical minimum and that axes are not reversed.
- Map an accessible switch/button to the simulator's arm/disarm function if the game supports it.
- Keep the real radio's antenna installed when powering the radio, as its manual requires. The simulator does not require a real receiver or powered quad.

Menu wording can vary by EdgeTX and operating-system versions. If input does not appear, troubleshoot USB mode, cable (some cables are charge-only), operating-system recognition, and axis calibration one at a time.

## A practical 30–45 minute session

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** look at the controls and practise small, deliberate movements with the aircraft stationary or in a safe training area.
2. **Single-axis drills (10 min):** practise gentle throttle changes; then roll only, pitch only, and yaw only where possible. Notice the effect each input has on orientation.
3. **Orientation (10 min):** fly slowly and keep the aircraft facing different directions. Avoid large inputs when its nose faces you.
4. **Circuit (10 min):** fly a large box or oval at a comfortable height, using smooth turns and consistent throttle.
5. **Landing and review (5–10 min):** approach a clear spot, reduce throttle progressively, disarm after landing, and write down one thing that went well and one thing to practise next time.

Quality of control matters more than total airtime. Stop if fatigued or frustrated.

## Suggested progression

### Level A — orientation and throttle
- [ ] Maintain a gentle, roughly consistent altitude.
- [ ] Make small pitch/roll/yaw inputs rather than full-stick corrections.
- [ ] Stop oscillating between opposite inputs.
- [ ] Take off and land in a chosen clear area.

### Level B — repeatable paths
- [ ] Fly a large rectangle in both directions.
- [ ] Fly a wide figure-eight without abrupt corrections.
- [ ] Keep turns coordinated and exit each turn pointed where intended.
- [ ] Recover calmly after losing orientation; reduce the speed of inputs rather than panicking.

### Level C — precision
- [ ] Fly through wide gates with margin on both sides.
- [ ] Repeat the same circuit several times with similar altitude and speed.
- [ ] Practise a planned landing approach from different directions.
- [ ] Review crashes: identify the control error and repeat the relevant drill before increasing speed.

### Level D — progression towards real flying
- [ ] Understand the chosen flight mode and its limitations.
- [ ] Understand arm/disarm behavior and how to stop motors promptly.
- [ ] Can fly several calm laps without repeated crashes in a familiar simulator environment.
- [ ] Can explain the reason for each crash in terms of orientation, throttle, overcorrection, or speed.

These are suggested coaching milestones, not a certification standard. Simulator physics, rates, camera angle, and aircraft setup differ from real life; continue cautiously and get experienced help before a first outdoor flight.

## Keep a session log

Record simulator, map, aircraft preset, flight mode, rates (if changed), practice drills, crashes and likely causes, and one objective for the next session. Change only one major setting at a time so the effect is understandable.
